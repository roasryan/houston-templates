Root Barrier Installation in Houston, TX

A big live oak can drink hundreds of gallons of water a day. When its roots reach under your slab, they dry out the clay and that side of the house drops. A root barrier keeps the roots out while keeping the tree.

Signs you may need root barriers

  • A large tree within 20 feet of the house
  • Cracks on the side closest to a tree
  • Roots showing at the slab edge
  • That side of the house drops more in summer
  • Lifted sidewalk or driveway near the tree
  • A neighbor’s tree close to your property line
  • Doors on that side stick in summer
  • Floor readings low near the tree

Good to know in Houston

Houston neighborhoods are full of mature oaks. The answer is usually a barrier, not cutting down the tree.

Barriers work best with steady watering and good drainage.

Questions about root barriers

Will a root barrier kill my tree?

A barrier redirects roots. We keep a safe distance and can bring in an arborist.

How deep does it go?

Usually 2 to 4 feet, based on the tree and soil.

Do I still need piers?

If the house already dropped a lot, maybe. The barrier stops it from getting worse.

Can I do it myself?

Small runs, maybe. Utility lines and depth make it a job for a crew.
