Massage questions, answered

General

How much is a massage in Houston?

Most Houston massages run about $80 to $140 for 60 minutes, depending on the spa and the type. Ours are $95 for Swedish and $110 for deep tissue.

Do I have to take my clothes off?

Undress only as far as you’re comfortable. You are draped with a sheet the whole time, and only the area being worked on is uncovered.

What is the first-visit form for?

We ask you to fill out a short consultation form before your first massage. It covers your health and the draping rules, and we keep it on file.

Are your therapists licensed?

Yes. Texas requires massage therapists to be licensed by TDLR, and massage establishments must hold a license too. Ours is posted in the lobby.

What if I need to cancel?

Cancel or reschedule at least 24 hours ahead for no charge. Later cancels are charged 50% and no-shows the full service.

Does a Swedish massage hurt?

Deep tissue

How much is a deep tissue massage in Houston?

Usually about $90 to $150 for 60 minutes. Ours is $110.

Will I be sore afterward?

You might be for a day. Warm water and light stretching help.

What is the difference between deep tissue and Swedish?

Deep tissue is firmer and focuses on tight spots. Swedish is lighter and more relaxing.

Can I ask for lighter pressure?

Yes, at any time. Speak up and your therapist will change it.

Sports and trigger point

How much is a sports massage in Houston?

Usually $95 to $150 for 60 minutes. Ours is $115.

Is sports massage only for athletes?

No. Anyone with recurring tight spots can book it.

Will it fix an injury?

No. A massage can relax muscles, but it does not diagnose or treat injuries. See a doctor for pain that is sharp or new.

How often should I book?

Many people go every 2 to 4 weeks while training.

Hot stone massage

How much is a hot stone massage in Houston?

Usually $100 to $220 depending on length. Ours is $165 for 90 minutes.

Are hot stones safe?

They are tested for temperature first. Tell your therapist if anything feels too hot.

Who should skip hot stone?

People with certain conditions, such as diabetes, poor circulation or skin issues, should check with a doctor first.

Do the stones hurt?

They should feel warm and smooth. Speak up if the heat or pressure is too much.
