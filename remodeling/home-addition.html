Home Additions and Structural Work

Home additions in Houston need a plan, a permit and sometimes an engineer. We scope each project in writing, pull City permits and use engineered beams and foundation work where the house calls for it.

When an addition or structural change makes sense

  • You need a bedroom, office or second living area
  • You want to open a wall between kitchen and living room
  • A room is too small for your family
  • You want a second story on a one-story home
  • A garage could become living space
  • The house needs a new beam or support
  • You want a larger primary suite
  • Selling costs more than adding space

Good to know in Houston

Houston’s expansive clay makes foundations a key part of every addition. Floodplain rules can limit what you can add and how high it must sit. In historic districts like the Heights, an addition may need design approval before a permit.

The City of Houston requires a building permit and inspections for additions. Floodplain lots, deed restrictions and historic districts can add rules. Structural changes need an engineer’s letter. Homestead contracts need both spouses’ signatures and the Property Code 53.255 disclosure.

Questions about additions and structural work

Do I need an engineer to remove a wall?

If the wall is load-bearing, yes. We confirm what the wall holds before we cut anything.

How long does an addition take?

Many take 3 to 6 months, including permits and inspections.

Will an addition raise my property taxes?

Probably, yes. The appraisal district may update your home’s value after a permitted addition.

Do I need approval from my HOA?

Most HOAs require plan approval before work begins.
